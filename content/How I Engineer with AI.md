I used to be very critical of building with AI, mainly because I'd fallen into that trap myself. Vibe coding: generating piles of code that felt productive right up until something broke or someone asked me to explain it, and I completely fell apart because I never actually understood what I'd made. That experience made me wary. For a while I couldn't tell whether using AI meant I wasn't really doing the work.

Building this project is where I resolved that. I found the balance that turns AI into a force multiplier instead of a crutch, and it comes down to a clear division of labor.

**I own all the architecture.** Every design decision, tradeoff, and direction is mine. I work the problem out first, across all layers: from the data model to the final product's philosophy. I ensure that I plan out every step of every layer before any code gets written.

**AI simply accelerates execution.** Once I've designed a solution and understand the pattern, I direct AI to implement it, and I review every result against one standard: can I explain this and change it? I write the first instance of a pattern by hand to internalize it and understand the why, then delegate the repetition. The novel, high-value work stays hand-written.

That's the balance that works. I built a real, full product faster than I could have alone, without ever feeling like I didn't do the work or didn't own it, because I made every decision that mattered and can stand behind every line that counts.

I've come to understand that this is what modern software engineering looks like: not writing every character yourself, and not shipping code you don't understand, but directing powerful tools with planning and judgment you own. That's the skill I built here, and it's the one I'm carrying forward in my career.
