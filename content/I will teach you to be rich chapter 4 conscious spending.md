- who wants to track their spending?
- forget budgeting, instead, create a conscious spending plan. what if you could make sure you were saving and investing enough money each month, and then use the rest of your money guilt-free for whatever you want? 
- you have to plan where you want your money to go ahead of time.
- automate your saving and investing and make your spending decisions crystal clear
- NO ONE STICKS TO STRICT BUDGETS
- there is power in saying no to the things we don't care about, but there is even more power in saying a big YES to the things we love
- Conscious spending isnt about cutting your spending on everything. that approach won't last. it's about choosing the things you love enough to spend extravagantly on, then cutting costs mercilessly on the things you don't love
- I want you to consciously decide what you're going to spend on. no more "i guess i spent that much" when you see bills. conscious spending means you decide exactly where you're going to spend your money-going out, saving, investing, rent- and free yourself from feeling guilty about your spending
- along with feeling comfortable with your spending, a plan keeps you moving toward your goals instead of just treading water.
- plan to spend on what's important to them, and save the rest
- to create your own conscious spending plan, start simiple and work to improve over time
- A concious spending plan involves four major buckets: fixed costs, investments, savings, and guilt-free spending money

### Long-term investments
- This bucket includes the amount youll send to your 401k and roth ira every month
- good rule of thumb is 10% of take-home pay after taxes.
- your 401 contributions count toward the 10%, so if you already contribute to one, add that amount to take-home money to get a monthly salary
- if youre not sure how much to allot for investing bucket, opne an investment calculator from bankrate.com and enter numbers. experiment with contributing different amounts. assuming an 8% return, you'll see drastic differences over 40 years. (add this to app?)

### Savings Goals

## Optimizing your conscious spending plan
- after working on the basics, you can make targeted improvements to tweak spending and make your money go where it wants to go. this plan will serve as a living, breathing system that signals when something's broken, not creating a cloud of worry over spending too much. if the alarms aren't going off, you don't have to spend time worrying
- Optimizing doesnt have to be overwhelming. you can do a 80/20 analysis, where 80% of your overspending is used only on 20% of your expenditures. focus on one or two big spending areas and solve those instead of focusing on small 5% changes.
- example, most expenses are predictable: rent, subway passes, etc. you could zoom in on the two or three spending areas that vary wildly that you'd like to control, like food, travel, clothes.
- google search for "conducting a pareto analysis"
- looking at expenses, like credit card payments. he could negotiate for a lower APR, save interest. unsubscribe from subscriptions youre not using. track how much money spent on food and lower. all of these save more money, and then you're able to use that money on things you love
- focus on things that matter, choose the big wins. instead of focusing on buying less cokes, focus on the big wins that really make an impact on total dollar amount. focus on big wins that are expenses that make you think "yeah i probably spend too much on that"

## Set realistic goals
- habits don't change overnight, and arent sustainable if they are
- start with bite sized changes, and work in increments from there
- start by making the smallest change, and follow your own plan for gradually increasing it.

## Use the envelope system to target big wins
- ramit reccomends the envelope system, where you allocate money for certain categories like eating out, shopping, rent, and so on. once you spend that allocated money, you cant spend anymore. in emergencies you can dip into other envelopes, but cut back until you replenish that envelope. they can be figurative (app?) or literal envelopes. - whatever system they choose to divvy up the money is fine, just decide how much you want to spend in major categories in each month (pick big wins to start). put allotted money in each envelope. when it's empty, that's it for the month. you can transfer from one envelope to another, but it's still a finite amount per month.

## Maintaining Your Spending Plan
- Once you've done what you can to design and inplement a Conscious Spending plan, give time to settle into a rhythm with it. as you go month to month, youll discover surprises you hadn't anticipated. unexpected cash expenses, don't flip if you miss tracking a few dollars, the minute your system becomes too oppressive is the minute you stop using it (important for app)
- takes time in the beginnign, but gets easier. make tracking spending a weekly priority

### How to handle unexpected and irregular expenses
- account for unexpected and build in flexibility
- known irregular events- vehicle registration fees, christmas gifts, vacations). theres an easy way to account for this, its already built into the spending plan: under savings goals, allocate money towards goals whre you have a general idea of how much it will cost. like if you know christmas will cost $500, start saving $42/month in january. by december, youll be prepared
- unknown irregular events- medical expenses, traffic tickets. these fall under monthly fixed expenses, as no matter how hard you try, there will always be unexpected expenses. earlier it was said to add 15% to estimated costs to accomodate for these surprises. in addition, save $50 a month for unexpected expenses. over time, adjust and get a better idea of what this figure should be for you and change accordingly. as time goes by, you'll get a much more accurate understanding of how to project. 
- unexpected one-time income- birthday gift, tax return, freelance contract. you don't have to save it all, ramit usually uses 50% of it for whatever. other half goes to investing, have fun! handling one-time income like this conscioulsy is more meaningful in short and long term
- Raises. when it comes to raises, it's okay to increase standard of living a bit, but bank the rest. you earned it, you should enjoy it.

The best part about seting up a strategic conscious spending plan is that it guides your decisions, letting you say no much more easily, and freeing you up to enjoy what you do spend on. this is guilt free spending at it's best. there will be hard choices, but this system makes it less painful. setting up this system will let you make the toughh decisions up front and spend your miney guilt-free.
